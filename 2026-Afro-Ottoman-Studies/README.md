- ## Abstract

This introductory article maps the emergence and institutional consolidation of Studies of Afro-Ottoman History within Turkish academia over six decades. Beginning with Cengiz Orhonlu’s pioneering efforts in the 1960s and Ahmet Kavas’s revitalisation in the early 2000s, supported by key institutions including İSAM, IRCICA, ORDAF, AFAM, and TTK, Turkish academia now produces multilingual scholarship involving several young researchers drawing from Ottoman Turkish, Arabic, English, and French sources. This article documents the chronology and genealogy of the last twenty-five years of research conducted in Turkey. Despite progress, colonial legacies persist through restrictive visa regimes and closed-access publishing that marginalise Turkish and African researchers. Works on Studies of Afro-Ottoman History in Turkey continue to remain out of reach of Anglophone research literature. This Special Issue bridges these divides by presenting three contributions in English that retain their multilingual source bases. Collectively, these articles recover previously marginalised African agency, challenge colonial historiography through local and Ottoman archives, and illuminate a five-century span of transcontinental connections between Istanbul and the African continent and African people.
