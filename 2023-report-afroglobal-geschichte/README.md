- ## Abstract

While the mainstream literature of global history has partly positioned itself as a successor to European imperial history with a new framework and partly as an explanation of the "globalized" present, critiques, particularly from an African historical perspective, have been able to make little impact on its deterministic and Eurocentric views. As a result, the two blind spots in the literature have been further underestimated. The first blind spot is that there is a great disinterest in the non-European global histories that were erased by colonial invasions, arguing that they have no determinative influence in today's "globalized" present. Despite introductory research literature in Turkish, Arabic and Persian on the global-historical connection between Africa, the Middle East and India before the 20th century, which was destroyed by colonialism, this topic has not yet found a place in the mainstream literature of global history.1 The second blind spot, the highly underestimated role of Africa in the present, which is seen as shaped by Eurocentric forces, remains an open question. In this context, this section is dedicated to providing introductory discussions and further theoretical formulations on this neglected topic.
